Our Story · Since 1901

I/ The Vow

Late Heera Jha, a respected barrister who graduated from Kolkata University in 1882, was once invited to the Jagadhatri Pooja at Chandannagar in Bengal. The priest told him — Maa Jagadhatri represents power and peace, and blesses her devotees with offspring.

Wishing for a successor, Heera Jha made a vow before the Goddess: "If you bless me with a son, I will worship you at my home with all fanfare, every year."

II/ The Blessing

At the time of immersion, as the idol of the Goddess was absorbed into the holy Ganges, the Jha couple stood with tears in their eyes, hearts full of devotion.

Right then, a flower of reverence fell into the lap of Mrs. Jha — a sign that their wish had been granted. In 1900, Late Kamlakant Jha was born.

III/ The Tradition

From the very next year, 1901, Maa Jagadhatri has been worshipped at the village of Saidapur, Godda (Jharkhand) — every single year, without fail, for over 125 years.

Kamlakant Jha (1900–1968) raised a family of six sons and a daughter, and today the whole Kamlakant Jha Family continues this sacred tradition, praying for the welfare of the whole world.

Upcoming · Kartik Shukla Paksha 2026

Maa Jagadhatri Pooja 2026

The Goddess descends upon the land of Saidapur with her whole family and accepts our offerings.

  1. 17Nov

    Maha Saptami Tuesday

    The festivities commence. The temple is adorned, devotional tunes of the band, clarinet and shehnai fill the air, and devotees begin their Sanyam (sacred fasting with a wish).

  2. 18Nov

    Maha Ashtami Wednesday

    Maa Jagadhatri descends upon Saidapur. The pandal comes alive with a huge crowd of devotees, and as the day progresses the village turns into a grand fair — stalls, toys, balloons, Ferris wheels and pirate boats.

  3. 19Nov

    Maha Navami · Akshaya Navami Thursday

    Trikala Pooja begins in the morning — the pooja process is Tantrik. Devotees on Sanyam remain in the temple; after the three poojas a Havan is performed and the Acharya distributes the offered flowers.

  4. 20Nov

    Visarjan · Ekadashi Friday

    The Visarjan pooja bids the Goddess farewell, and the idols are immersed in the Kajhiya river with a grand procession. Afterwards, juniors bow to elders and receive blessings for a bright and healthy future.

FAQs

Questions devotees ask us

What are the dates of Maa Jagadhatri Pooja 2026?

Maha Saptami — 17 Nov, Maha Ashtami — 18 Nov, Maha Navami — 19 Nov 2026. The Visarjan (immersion) takes place on Ekadashi, 20 November 2026, with a grand procession to the Kajhiya river.

Where exactly does the pooja take place?

At Maa Jagadhatri Mandir, Saidapur, District Godda, Jharkhand – 814133. Saidapur is well connected within Godda district; devotees from across the region travel here every year.

How old is this pooja, and why is it celebrated?

Celebrated every year since 1901, it commemorates the birth of Late Kamlakant Jha, son of Late Heera Jha — fulfilling a vow made to Maa Jagadhatri at Chandannagar. It is one of the oldest poojas in the region.

Who is Maa Jagadhatri?

Maa Jagadhatri — "the bearer of the world" — represents power and peace, rides a lion, and blesses her devotees with offspring and prosperity. According to the scriptures, she comes to Saidapur with her whole family and accepts our offerings.

What happens on Maha Navami?

On Akshaya Navami, Trikala Pooja starts in the morning and the pooja process is Tantrik. Devotees observing Sanyam (fasting with a specific wish) stay in the temple. After all three poojas, a Havan is performed and the Acharya distributes the offered flowers — many families have stories of these blessings coming true.

Is there a fair for families and children?

Yes! As the day progresses, the gathering becomes a grand fair — stalls of toys and balloons, fun rides like Ferris wheels and pirate boats. The whole village becomes an extended family, and the police administration is present to keep everyone safe.

When is the immersion, and what is unique about it?

More than a decade ago, a divine storm delayed a night immersion — and the idols were immersed the next day, on Ekadashi, with a grand, unprecedented procession. That became the tradition: immersion in the Kajhiya river on Ekadashi, followed by juniors bowing to elders for blessings.
